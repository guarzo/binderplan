# Review, Keeper, and Trade Binder Design

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Status:** Approved in conversation; card assignments remain proposed until physically sorted.

## Purpose

Separate four jobs that the former holding box was performing at once:

1. Preserve the canonical thematic thesis in Volumes I and II.
2. Keep active candidates easy to compare during audits.
3. Display cards that are permanently wanted despite having no thematic home.
4. Make valuable cards that are actively available easy to transport and trade.

The design does not increase canonical capacity by weakening existing themes. Volume II retains the two-page theme cap. A card enters canon only by defeating a named incumbent or by helping a new theme pass the existing admission gates.

## Collection architecture

### 1. Canonical binders: Volumes I and II

- Existing themes remain capped at one or two pages.
- Every existing pocket is occupied.
- An incoming card must displace a named incumbent.
- A new Volume II theme requires a strong nine-card page immediately and a credible path to a second page.
- Three-page themes remain an anti-pattern because they dilute selection pressure.

### 2. Review binder: nine-pocket

The Review binder contains cards with either a specific live curatorial hypothesis or a known canonical fit deliberately retained as rotation stock. Uncertainty by itself does not earn a Review pocket.

Front-to-back divider order:

1. **EDGE Watches**
   - Unease
   - Environmental Momentum
   - No general unnamed watch cluster: a card needs a stated hypothesis.
2. **Existing-theme EDGE**
   - Volume II challenges first, then Volume I.
   - Grouped by the canonical theme challenged.
3. **REDUNDANT**
   - Valid fits that have already lost a comparison.
   - Grouped by canonical theme.
4. **FUTURE SELF**
   - A timed cooling-off lane for cards that remain personally interesting but unresolved.
5. **Event Staging**
   - Temporary outbound queue at the back of the binder.
   - Cards selected for an event retain their underlying Review classification.
   - Unswapped cards return to their exact original sections.

#### Review classification rules

- **EDGE Watch:** satisfies the strict visual rule of Unease or Environmental Momentum.
- **Existing-theme EDGE:** has a precise target theme and a plausible page challenge not yet decided.
- **REDUNDANT:** clean thematic fit that has already lost to the current page and is deliberately retained as rotation stock. The periodic binder audit is its retest trigger; cards not worth retaining for rotation move to Keeper, Trade, or Release.
- **FUTURE SELF:** expires after roughly one year into Keeper, Trade, or Release.
- A temporary intake sleeve or envelope may be used for up to 30 days, but intake is not a durable binder section.

### Active Volume II watch clusters

#### Unease

**Rule:** the setting or composition creates ambient tension without an attack, a dominant threatening subject, or explicit spectacle.

**Excludes:** direct menace, combat, generic Ghost/Dark imagery, and cards that depend on species reputation.

The current pool has two strict candidates. It is not ready for canon.

#### Environmental Momentum

**Rule:** wind, current, terrain, or open flight visibly determines the Pokémon's movement.

**Excludes:** joy-driven play, active combat, energy gathering, static elemental portraits, and visible boundary crossings.

The current pool has four strict candidates. It is not ready for canon.

#### Rejected watch: Uncanny / Glitch

The current cluster is effectively a Ditto mini-run. It does not provide independent evidence for a Volume II emotional theme. Ditto cards belong in Keeper/Species Studies or Beautiful Misfits instead.

## 3. Keeper binder: four-pocket

The Keeper binder contains stable, non-trade cards that are wanted independently of thematic placement. Four-pocket pages distinguish it visually from the canonical collection and give each card more presence.

Sections:

1. **Personal Keepers** — cards retained because of personal attachment, including unresolved favorites known to be permanent.
2. **Beautiful Misfits** — strong or unusual art with no useful thematic home.
3. **Heritage** — era, printing history, release context, and nostalgia.
4. **Species Studies** — deliberate bounded runs such as Houndour/Houndoom, Gengar, Ditto, or Emolga-related material.

Sequence visually inside each section by palette, scale, composition, or era rather than alphabetically.

The superseded first-sort proposal contained 46 Keeper cards. That historical capacity estimate remains useful for the proposed architecture, but the current 100-card inventory has not yet been fully reclassified. Preserving those historical section boundaries would require thirteen four-pocket pages (52 pockets), leaving six open pockets.

## 4. Trade binder: four-pocket

The Trade binder is portable and contains only cards explicitly available now.

- Permanent core: cards considered too valuable to release but available for trade.
- Event supplements: selected temporarily from Review before an event.
- Merely being in Review does not make a card tradeable.
- EDGE Watch cards do not enter Trade without a fresh curatorial decision.
- Unswapped event supplements return to their original Review sections.

The superseded first-sort proposal contained ten Core Value candidates: three four-pocket pages, leaving two open pockets. The owner later confirmed that none of the photographed cards is currently designated available for Trade.

## 5. Release group

Release is not a binder section. It is a separate, clearly labeled outbound group for cards that have no canonical path, permanent keeper reason, or value-based trade role.

The superseded first-sort proposal contained three Release candidates. Only Yveltal — Steam Siege 65 was later confirmed physically removed; the other proposed Release cards remain in the photographed holding inventory.

## Physical sorting sequence

1. Prepare dividers and empty pages before removing cards from the current box.
2. Pull the three Release cards into a separate review envelope.
3. Build Keeper/Heritage and Keeper/Species Studies first; these are the most stable assignments.
4. Build Trade/Core Value and confirm every card is actively available.
5. Build the Review binder by exact section and target theme.
6. Leave Event Staging empty until an event is scheduled.
7. Photograph every completed section and record exceptions against the manifest.
8. Reconcile any low-confidence identity before assigning a durable registry ID.

## Boundaries and failure prevention

- Review must not become attractive long-term storage.
- Keeper status is permanent ownership intent, not a consolation category.
- Trade requires explicit availability, not merely redundancy.
- Heritage is based on the reason for retention, not a claim that old cards belong in canon.
- Species Studies are runs, not themes, and cannot justify Volume II expansion.
- Existing completionist side collections remain authoritative when physical identity supports membership.
- This design deliberately adopts a stricter new-theme threshold than the evergreen 6–9-card minimum: for future Volume II growth, require nine strong cards immediately plus a credible second-page path. The evergreen theme documentation should be updated if this operating rule is adopted permanently.
- No new Volume II theme is admitted from the current pool.
